\subsection{Useful parallelism is bounded by duplicated state and selection}

Once query parallelism is sufficient, staging/selection/merge overhead can dominate (\cref{fig:7-batch}). Vogue and BGE expose missing absolute-work and decomposition features (\cref{sec:7-2,sec:7-6}). A richer scheduler must also repay calibration; integrated results do not isolate shared bounds.

\subsection{Execution speed changes the value of avoiding refinement}

Consider a latency model with fixed non-refinement cost $T_0>0$,
high-/low-budget refinement times $F_h\geq F_\ell>0$, and a common
refinement-time multiplier $0<\eta\leq1$. The low-budget speedup and
its derivatives are
\begin{align}
 S(\eta,T_0)&=\frac{T_0+\eta F_h}{T_0+\eta F_\ell},
 \label{eq:8-refinement-sensitivity}\\
 \frac{\partial S}{\partial\eta}
 &=\frac{T_0(F_h-F_\ell)}{(T_0+\eta F_\ell)^2}\geq0,\qquad
 \frac{\partial S}{\partial T_0}
 =\frac{\eta(F_\ell-F_h)}{(T_0+\eta F_\ell)^2}\leq0.\nonumber
\end{align}
For $F_h>F_\ell$, faster refinement reduces the value of cutting its
budget; lower fixed cost increases it. The derivatives hold with
non-refinement work fixed. Budget-dependent scan maintenance and
resource transitions require the controlled comparisons of
\cref{sec:7-5,sec:7-stream-cost}. Quality and reuse remain constraints
(\cref{tab:7-budget-full,fig:7-budget-risk}).
