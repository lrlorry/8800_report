For routed $U(q)$, the refinement multiplier sets \cite{jhq}
\begin{equation}
 c(\alpha)=\max\{k,\lceil\alpha k\rceil\},\qquad
 C_\alpha(q)=\operatorname{Top}^{\rm primary}_{\min\{c(\alpha),|U(q)|\}}U(q).
 \label{eq:6-budget}
\end{equation}
Require $|C_\alpha|\geq k$ for the analysis. Underfull slots are sentinels. Budgets change retention; probes change $U$.

The $k=10$ grid retains 20--2,000 survivors without retraining, trading residual reads against missed neighbours. Stored residuals and previously grown workspace remain allocated.

At fixed batch/routing, write
\begin{equation}
 T(\alpha)=T_{\rm base}+T_{\rm select}(c(\alpha))+
 T_{\rm refine}(c(\alpha))+T_{\rm final}(c(\alpha)).
 \label{eq:6-cost-model}
\end{equation}
$T_{\rm base}$ covers transfers, rotation, routing and primary arithmetic; budget-dependent selection/compaction follows \cref{fig:6-budget-scope}. Capacity/resource thresholds prevent a monotone-latency claim. The rule minimises accepted budget, not noisy QPS (\cref{sec:7-budget-trace}).

Every trial reruns rotation, probes and primary scoring. Index reuse avoids training/building, rather than caching query intermediates. Calibration therefore counts complete searches.

One budget serves the index, route, $k$ and workload. It is not a per-query predictor. Scan scheduling remains separate (\cref{sec:5-5}) and can change the calibration path.

\paragraph{Position among adaptive ANN methods.}
$\alpha$ serves the candidate-expansion role of Faiss's \texttt{k\_factor} and cuVS refinement \cite{faiss_refine,cuvs_refinement}. Alternatives use GT tuning \cite{faiss_autotune}, learned per-query stopping \cite{li_termination} or predicted recall \cite{darth}. Here unlabelled agreement chooses one reusable workload budget. Scores still use reconstructed vectors, unlike the cuVS source-vector refinement example.
