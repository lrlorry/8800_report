\frontchapter{Abstract}
JHQ-GPU studies how to execute JHQ's compressed primary/residual search efficiently within GPU memory. For fixed computed primary scores, grouped retention, merging and witnessed shared bounds preserve global top-$c$ selection under the stated ordering and memory-access conditions. Cartesian splitting replaces 256 LUT entries with $16+16$, giving a storage ratio $32/256=1/8$ with the same real-arithmetic score. Calibration compares sampled top-$k$ IDs with a larger-budget reference, without GT or a learned predictor.

On six RTX 5090 datasets, two-group LUTs lead in 22/24 cells; the largest index holds 17.78 million vectors at 20.44\,GiB resident memory. Full-pool calibration meets a $10^{-3}$ held-out
recall-loss target relative to the best tested budget in 12/12 cells, with
maximum loss 0.0006; smaller samples expose workload-dependent quality risk.
At recall 0.97 and batch 1,000, JHQ leads in three of four datasets and is
near parity with IVF-RaBitQ on arXiv. OpenAI runs use TF32; FP32 checks
retain favourable comparisons under a TF32-trained cache. At batch 8 and
the same recall, CAGRA fp32 leads on all four datasets. These results
locate the execution, quality and capacity trade-offs of GPU JHQ.
