\subsection{Transform and sampling}

Host QR factorisation of a seeded Gaussian matrix produces square $\Pi$:
\begin{equation}
 Y_t=X_t\Pi^{\mathsf T},\qquad y=\Pi x,\qquad
 \|\Pi(q-x)\|_2^2=\|q-x\|_2^2
 \quad\text{when }\Pi^{\mathsf T}\Pi=I.
 \label{eq:4-transform}
\end{equation}
The geometry contract (C0) checks finite-precision QR/GEMM error;
dimension remains $d$ (\cref{sec:4-6}).

Strided primary/IVF training takes $n_t=39n_{\rm list}$:
\[
 n_t=\begin{cases}159{,}744,&\text{Vogue},\\319{,}488,&\text{arXiv/OpenAI},\\1{,}277{,}952,&\text{BGE/Stella}.\end{cases}
\]
This is Faiss's warning threshold, not a quality guarantee \cite{faiss_clustering}. Residual fitting uses 100,000 rows.

An older twelve-cell Vogue/OpenAI-3072 sweep ($B_r=4/8$, probes 32/128/256) fixed primary/IVF models. With 25 Lloyd iterations, 100K rather than all rows lost at most 0.0018 recall (eleven losses/one tie). Intermediate sizes were non-monotone; this supports bounded training only within that protocol.

\subsection{Analytical primary codebook}

Sample scale and Cartesian levels are
\begin{equation}
 \sigma^2=\frac{1}{n_t d}\sum_{i=0}^{n_t-1}\|x_i\|_2^2,
 \qquad D_s=d/M,\qquad K=2^B.
 \label{eq:4-scale}
\end{equation}
\[
 B=D_s=8\Longrightarrow
 \mathcal C_m=\{-a,+a\}^{8},\quad a=0.674\sigma.
\]
Eight signs fit a byte, enabling $O(d)$ encoding/split LUTs \cite{jhq} (\cref{sec:5-2}). Arbitrary Lloyd updates may break Cartesian structure.

For fixed sign vector $s\in\{-1,+1\}^d$,
\begin{equation}
 \|q'-a s\|^2=\|q'\|^2+da^2-2a\langle q',s\rangle.
 \label{eq:4-sign-ranking}
\end{equation}
\[
 a>0\Longrightarrow
 \arg\min_s\|q'-as\|^2=\arg\max_s\langle q',s\rangle.
\]
The order needs $a>0$, not Gaussian data. Residuals depend on $a$; zero scale/FP32 ties are separate cases.



\subsection{Coarse assignment as filter and refine}

IVF initialises spaced rotated rows and runs eight iterations using $s_\ell=\|\mu_\ell\|^2-2\langle y,\mu_\ell\rangle$. Supported dimensions use int8 filtering, resolving ambiguity in FP32.

\begin{resultblock}
\begin{lemma}[Assignment with certified score intervals]
\label{lem:4-assignment-bound}
Let $\widetilde y,\widetilde\mu_\ell$ be dequantised approximations,
$e=y-\widetilde y$ and $f_\ell=\mu_\ell-\widetilde\mu_\ell$.
Keeping centroid norms exact, the approximate score
$\widetilde s_\ell=\|\mu_\ell\|^2-2\langle\widetilde y,\widetilde\mu_\ell\rangle$
has real-arithmetic error bounded by
\begin{equation}
 E_\ell=2\bigl(\|y\|\|f_\ell\|+\|e\|\|\widetilde\mu_\ell\|\bigr).
 \label{eq:4-assignment-bound}
\end{equation}
Given valid intervals $[L_\ell,U_\ell]$ containing every target score,
discarding only $L_\ell>\min_j U_j$ and exactly resolving the remaining
scores preserves the minimum and its specified tie rule.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Using $e=y-\widetilde y$ and $f_\ell=\mu_\ell-\widetilde\mu_\ell$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle y,\mu_\ell\rangle-
 \langle\widetilde y,\widetilde\mu_\ell\rangle
 &=\langle y,f_\ell\rangle
   +\langle e,\widetilde\mu_\ell\rangle,\\
 |s_\ell-\widetilde s_\ell|
 &\leq2\bigl(\|y\|\|f_\ell\|
       +\|e\|\|\widetilde\mu_\ell\|\bigr)=E_\ell .
 \end{aligned}
\]
For $j^*\in\arg\min_j U_j$,
\[
 L_\ell>U_{j^*}
 \Longrightarrow
 s_\ell\geq L_\ell>U_{j^*}\geq s_{j^*}.
\]
The strict inequality excludes $\ell$; equality stays eligible.
\end{proof}
\end{resultblock}

FP32 headroom lacks a bitwise certificate without accumulation-error bounds. FP16 differs on 0/14,838 Vogue, 6/31,748 BGE and 3/16,359 Stella tail assignments. Cold logs do not identify precision.

\subsection{Residual fitting and the iteration budget}

Residuals stay $r=\Pi x-\widehat p(x)$. Per subspace, GPU sorting/Lloyd fits $K_r=2^{B_r}$ sorted scalar levels shared across coordinates, capped at 2,000 updates.

Historical 100K training gained 0.0063--0.0076 recall at 25$\to$2,000 updates. Tail-bin/BGE/Stella changes persisted: the cap is not convergence, without contradicting exact Lloyd descent. At 20,000 updates, historical Stella all-row/100K recall was 0.9921/0.9920.

\subsection{Construction work and observed imbalance}

\[
 W_{\rm QR}=O(d^3),\qquad W_{\rm rotate}=O(n_t d^2).
\]
Direct coarse assignment costs $O(n_t n_{\rm list}d)$ per iteration.
\[
 W_{\rm primary}=O(MKD_s),\qquad
 W_{\rm residual\ extraction}=O(n_rd),
\]
followed by sorting and scalar updates. These describe work, rather than predict latency.
Synchronised phase diagnostics place about 83\% of BGE/Stella training
in IVF k-means (about 2.97\,s). They use one add buffer and forced
synchronisation, separately from the normal overlapped timings in
\cref{sec:7-7}.


List-size imbalance enters the scan rule in \cref{sec:5-5}. The
individual contributions of eight IVF iterations, spaced seeds and
finite sampling to that imbalance were not isolated.


\paragraph{Arithmetic used in the measured implementation.}
The evaluated implementation (v69) stores FP32 matrices but enables cuBLAS
TF32 tensor-core math for $d\geq1{,}536$. In its compiled faithful branch,
a null override falls through to the dimension-based default. This affects
training/add/query rotation and centroid GEMMs on both OpenAI datasets;
an environment override cannot disable that branch. Thus FP32 storage does
not imply full FP32 multiplication. The supplementary FP32 variant (v72)
corrects this switch. Its sensitivity experiment reuses the same
TF32-trained cache and changes add/query arithmetic, rather than retraining
the model in FP32 (\cref{sec:7-precision}).
