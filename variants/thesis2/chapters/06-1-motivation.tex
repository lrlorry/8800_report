For routed $U(q)$, the refinement multiplier sets \cite{jhq}
\begin{equation}
 c(\alpha)=\max\{k,\lceil\alpha k\rceil\},\qquad
 C_\alpha(q)=\operatorname{Top}^{\rm primary}_{\min\{c(\alpha),|U(q)|\}}U(q).
 \label{eq:6-budget}
\end{equation}
Analysis requires $|C_\alpha|\geq k$; underfull slots are sentinels. Budgets change retention, probes $U$.

At $k=10$, the grid retains 20--2,000 survivors without retraining. Lower budgets save reads, not residual storage or previously reserved workspace.

At fixed batch/routing, write
\begin{equation}
 T(\alpha)=T_{\rm base}+T_{\rm select}(c(\alpha))+
 T_{\rm refine}(c(\alpha))+T_{\rm final}(c(\alpha)).
 \label{eq:6-cost-model}
\end{equation}
$T_{\rm base}$ covers transfers/rotation/routing/primary arithmetic (\cref{fig:6-budget-scope}). Selection/resource transitions make latency non-monotone; the rule minimises accepted budget (\cref{sec:7-budget-trace}).

Trials reuse the index but rerun full searches, including rotation/probing/primary scoring; calibration costs more than residual work alone.



\paragraph{Position among adaptive ANN methods.}
$\alpha$ serves the candidate-expansion role of Faiss's \texttt{k\_factor} and cuVS refinement \cite{faiss_refine,cuvs_refinement}. Alternatives use GT tuning \cite{faiss_autotune}, learned per-query stopping \cite{li_termination} or predicted recall \cite{darth}. Here unlabelled agreement chooses one reusable workload budget. Scores still use reconstructed vectors, unlike the cuVS source-vector refinement example.
