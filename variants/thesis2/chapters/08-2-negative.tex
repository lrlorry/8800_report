\subsection{Mailman-style shared sums: arithmetic reuse meets scatter cost}
\label{sec:8-mailman}

Mailman reuses finite-alphabet sums \cite{mailman}. Frozen Vogue/arXiv/OpenAI-3072 streams compare atomic buckets/private histograms at widths 4/6/8 with packed two-table scoring: 128 probes, batches 8/1,000, fixed codes/mathematical scores.

The best alternatives reach only 0.187--0.581 of baseline QPS (\cref{fig:8-negative}). Biased bucket destinations dominate profiles; private histograms add state/shared updates. Narrower groups sometimes help, but none wins. Other finite-alphabet designs remain open.

Only scores were tested, so cutoff checks do not establish neighbour-pipeline equivalence. The replacement model gives
\[
 \frac{T'}T=1+f\left(\frac1s-1\right)>1
 \quad(0<f\leq1,\ 0<s<1).
\]
This model is not measured end-to-end performance; integration stopped. A dictionary study also stopped at structural statistics. Neither contributes a system-frontier point.

\subsection{Rank-oriented coding: a local gain is not a general replacement}
\label{sec:8-rank}

FP64 candidate replay uses 400/200/400 calibration/\allowbreak{}validation/\allowbreak{}test queries. It tests weighted scores, median/list thresholds, learned losses and linear-MMSE decoding. Lower MSE can worsen neighbour order; primary-level changes also move residual reference points.

Median thresholds reduce arXiv's minimum tested qualifying count 875$\to$375; two other workloads fail. For orthogonal $\Pi$,
\[
 \bar y=\frac1N\sum_i\Pi x_i=\Pi\bar x,
 \qquad \|\bar y\|^2=\bar x^\top\Pi^\top\Pi\bar x=\|\bar x\|^2.
\]
Rotation therefore preserves mean-vector norm rather than removing bias. Centring must also preserve the refined-score ceiling.

Median-level GPU follow-up retrains residuals. ArXiv's original $\alpha=100$ target gains $1.40/1.21\times$ at batch 8/1,000; Vogue reaches 0.9630 below 0.9644, OpenAI-3072 ties. Three rounds use separate states/queries, outside E1.
