\paragraph{IVF-PQ.}

\paragraph{IVF-RaBitQ and brute force.}

16/\allowbreak{}32/\allowbreak{}64/\allowbreak{}96/\allowbreak{}128/\allowbreak{}192/\allowbreak{}256/\allowbreak{}384/\allowbreak{}512/\allowbreak{}768/\allowbreak{}1,024. The 768-dimensional and
OpenAI-1536 records contain LUT16/LUT32/QUANT4/QUANT8 modes;
OpenAI-3072 contains LUT32/QUANT4/QUANT8. Additional 4,096-list OpenAI
indexes extend the default 8,192-list coverage. Exact combinations are
in the registry. Each dataset also has one FP32 squared-L2 brute-force
point. An absent mode or higher-recall point is not a measured failure.

\paragraph{Auxiliary grids.}
LUT evaluation fixes $\alpha=100$ and probes 8/\allowbreak{}32/\allowbreak{}128/\allowbreak{}512 on six

Budget evaluation uses probes 128/\allowbreak{}512 and

\section{Secondary Results and Evidence Status}
\label{sec:b-secondary}
\paragraph{Adverse regimes.}
The scheduler's largest recorded regret is $1.217\times$ at batch 256.
Scheduled/one-block throughput on Vogue spans 0.895--1.175 in E1:
the skew rule enabling its recall-0.97 lead also adds overhead in some
low-probe cells (\cref{sec:7-6}). These adverse results accompany the
favourable internal scheduling findings. In T5's cross-system comparison,
CAGRA fp32 leads on all four datasets at recall 0.97 and batch 8;
batch-32 near ties depend on the recall estimator
(\cref{sec:7-smallbatch}).

\paragraph{Representation alternatives.}
Mailman-style GPU scorers are isolated kernel measurements; their
end-to-end replacements are optimistic models. The rank-oriented study
is an FP64 offline replay. A separate median-code GPU follow-up improves
arXiv at the recorded target by $1.402\times$ at batch 8 and
$1.208\times$ at batch 1,000, misses Vogue's strict target and is nearly
tied on OpenAI-3072. Thresholds, levels and residual training change
together, preventing a threshold-only attribution (\cref{sec:8-2}).

\paragraph{Calibration and precision.}
Finite-pool prediction explains sampling risk conditional on stable
query counts; its GT-loss mapping uses held-out fixed-arm quality.
Direct reuse measures cumulative cost on four repeated pools.
The precision intervention changes add/query GEMMs on one TF32-trained
cache. Full FP32 retraining, future-query drift, cross-device rankings
and online updates are outside these experiments.

\subsection{Contemporaneous precision anchors}
\label{sec:b-precision-anchors}

T1 is a later campaign than D: it remeasures JHQ and competing baselines
near recall 0.97 in one session, with the E1 workspace pools enabled.
Each JHQ configuration has two repetitions bracketing the baselines;
each repetition uses five timing rounds. Per-configuration QPS combines
the two round medians geometrically. There are twelve paired configurations
per OpenAI dataset. The FP32/TF32 geometric means are 0.956/0.964;
both variants reuse a TF32-trained cache and change add/query arithmetic.

\section{Artifact Access}
\label{sec:b-artifacts}
The report repository contains the parameter registry, derived tables,
figure sources, analysis scripts and 209-file v71 source/measurement
bundle under \path{evidence/ch5/v71/}. The offline command
\texttt{python3}\ \path{evidence/ch5/v71/check.py} verifies that bundle.
Selected T4/T5, T1 and A2 source/record inputs are also mirrored in
\path{evidence/review/}. Its checker verifies hashes and can rerun the
two corresponding offline audits using that local source root.
The broader raw records remain in the sibling \texttt{JHQ\_GPU} project
at the ledger paths. Complete vectors, trained states and all executables
are not packaged here. Offline recomputation requires the stated inputs;
GPU reproduction also requires the corresponding build and environment.
