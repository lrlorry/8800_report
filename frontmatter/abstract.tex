\frontchapter{Abstract}
Efficient GPU approximate nearest-neighbour search requires compact
representations, efficient scoring and selection, and suitable refinement
effort. This thesis presents
JHQ-GPU, a GPU implementation and evaluation of JHQ's inherited Cartesian
primary/residual hierarchy for high-dimensional squared-L2 search.

The system combines GPU training and batched index construction with
representation-aware query execution. Cartesian structure decomposes each
256-entry primary distance table into two 16-entry tables without changing
the real-arithmetic represented distance. Packed access, lane-cooperative
refinement and workload-dependent grouped scanning complement this design.
Selection and shared-bound arguments specify correctness conditions,
backed by a strict computed-score reference. A workload-level policy selects a
refinement multiplier through unlabelled output agreement with a larger
reference budget; its analysis separates sampled agreement from GT recall.

Archived measurements on an RTX 5090 cover six embedding datasets. The
largest tested index searches 17.78 million vectors at 20.44\,GiB resident
device memory. Two-group tables lead in 22/24 configurations. Integrated
scheduling improves geometric-mean throughput by $2.727\times$ at batch 8
against the lane-refinement reference, with much smaller full-batch gains.
At recall 0.97 and batch 1,000, interpolated comparisons place JHQ first
in three E1 datasets and near parity with IVF-RaBitQ on arXiv. OpenAI E1 uses TF32. FP32 interventions under a TF32-trained cache retain
numerical advantages, with cross-session comparisons distinguished from
contemporaneous anchors; neither experiment fully retrains FP32 models.
At batch 8 and the same recall, CAGRA fp32 instead leads on all four
datasets; batch-32 rankings also depend on the finite tuning grid.

Full-pool calibration meets a $10^{-3}$ held-out recall-loss target relative
to the best tested budget in all twelve cells, with maximum observed loss
0.0006. Smaller $S=128$ samples have up to 40/64 threshold crossings.
These fixed-pool measurements provide no population recall guarantee.
Direct reuse measurements support net savings on suitable workloads. Negative designs
show that arithmetic reuse and lower reconstruction error need not improve
the search frontier. The findings support joint evaluation of representation,
execution and effort under explicit quality, timing and capacity conditions.
