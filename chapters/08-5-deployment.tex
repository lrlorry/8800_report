JHQ-GPU merits evaluation when a compressed resident hierarchy fits the
device and the required recall lies within its measured competitive region.
E1 supports high-recall throughput at batch 1,000; T5 instead favours
CAGRA fp32 at recall 0.97 and batch 8 on all four datasets. At batch 32,
OpenAI-3072 is close under interpolation and favourable to JHQ under the
measured threshold; the other three favour CAGRA. Lower-recall JHQ
advantages also depend on dataset and grid. Grouped scheduling helps
JHQ internally without guaranteeing a cross-system lead
(\cref{sec:7-smallbatch}).

The recorded FULL policy is the quality-first calibration starting point,
followed by validation on the deployment's queries and direct reuse timing.
Compare quality, total time and resident memory under the actual batch
regime. The single-client synchronous experiment does not establish
concurrent-service tail latency, online-update performance or a population
recall guarantee.
