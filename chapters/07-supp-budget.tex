\subsection{Complete decision traces and finite-pool risk}
\label{sec:7-budget-trace}

The supplementary harness preserves the original grid, trained state,
query split and seeded draws, adding complete records. All twelve
fixed-arm recalls reproduce at the archived precision; full-pool choices,
sampled median choices and crossing counts reproduce. Timing changes the
GT oracle's fastest arm in two near-tied cells, illustrating that this
diagnostic also depends on measured speed.

All 2,304 sampled disagreement curves are monotone and their selected
budgets match enumeration. The twelve full-pool curves also pass both
checks. Moreover, summing full-pool per-query counts over each recorded
sample's actual IDs reproduces all 18,432 grid counts. This is stronger
than checking the selected budget alone, and supports using
\cref{eq:6-finite-pool} for these observations despite the scan-path
changes. It checks aggregate counts on the traced subsets, rather than
per-query invariance on every possible subset.


At $S=128$, maxima reach 0.0050 for arXiv/512 and 0.0046 for BGE/512;
at $S=32$ the largest is 0.0294. A low mean loss therefore coexists
with occasional materially worse chosen budgets. FULL's maximum across
the twelve cells remains 0.0006.

For arXiv/128, the finite-pool model predicts crossing probabilities
0.965, 0.875 and 0.620 at $S=32,64,128$, against 0.984, 0.875 and
0.625 observed (\cref{fig:7-finite-pool}). Acceptance uses only reference
outputs; translating its selection probabilities into GT-loss crossings
uses the fixed-arm held-out quality map. The calculation is therefore an
offline risk explanation, not a label-free GT guarantee. Across the 36
comparisons, the largest absolute gap is 0.144 for BGE/512 at $S=128$
(0.519 predicted, 0.375 observed). The fixed-pool calculation isolates
sampling risk without fitting a population model to held-out recall gaps.

Ten alternating remeasurements also remove the apparent small-budget
speed inversion. OpenAI-3072/128 gives 142.3/142.7 kQPS at
$\alpha=4/2$, versus 145.0/128.7 in the original short timing. BGE/128
and Vogue/512 likewise approach a plateau. This supports treating the
archived inversions as timing variation; it does not imply monotone
runtime for all budgets or resource thresholds.

\subsection{Direct calibration-plus-reuse measurement}
\label{sec:7-stream-cost}

Four cells directly execute fixed 64, fixed 100, sampled $S=128$ and
FULL policies, rotating policy order over five rounds. Each calibrated
round gathers its sample, calibrates once, explicitly installs the chosen
multiplier, then times 500 consecutive held-out batches. The first batch
includes any graph recapture. The same held-out pool is replayed, rather
than sampling new traffic. Index construction, prior warm-up and the
separately timed schedule calibration are outside this boundary.
\Cref{tab:7-stream-cost} reports the cumulative measurements.

On OpenAI-3072/128, the sampled policy selects 4 in every round and
reduces total time from 2,757 to 1,769\,ms against fixed 100, about
36\%, at the same measured recall 0.9252. FULL selects the same budget
and takes 1,783\,ms including its larger calibration cost. On
OpenAI-1536/128, FULL takes 860 versus 1,491\,ms at the same recall
0.9280; the sampled policy averages 852\,ms and 0.9276 recall.

The steady-rate fit to the sampled stream gives break-even counts of
3.6/5.5 batches against fixed 100/64 on OpenAI-1536, and 3.5/8.9 on
OpenAI-3072. These fractional values fit directly timed calibration and
steady batches; they are not directly observed fractional crossings.
\Cref{fig:7-stream-cost} instead plots measured cumulative totals,
including first-batch setup. The first positive mean cumulative savings
occur at 4/6 batches on OpenAI-1536 and 4/9 on OpenAI-3072 against
fixed 100/64. BGE/512 has a smaller sampled saving,
4,921 versus 5,185\,ms against fixed 100, but mean recall 0.9658 versus
0.9670; its FULL policy restores 0.9664 at 5,062\,ms. ArXiv's FULL
policy selects 200 for higher quality and is slower than fixed 100/64,
both of which miss the quality target. Thus timing and quality jointly
determine whether calibration is worthwhile. The measurements support
reuse economics without requiring the old fitted payback values to
match exactly in every cell.
