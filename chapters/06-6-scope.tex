Reuse is justified only for a sufficiently stable index, $k$, routing
configuration and query population. Changing the index or routing changes
the calibration target; a different batch regime may also change ties and
arithmetic through scheduling. Interpreting agreement requires valid,
unique output IDs, held-out GT quality and measured tail loss. Reuse
additionally requires common ordering or matched enumeration, repeated
timing and enough subsequent work to recover calibration cost
(\cref{sec:6-3,sec:6-5}). These are applicability conditions; the harness
does not automatically detect workload changes.


For the observed workload family, the resulting choice is full-pool
calibration when quality takes priority, with $S=128$ as the cheaper
variant whose crossing risk is measured separately. A caller must install
the selected $\alpha$ explicitly and reuse it with the same index, route
and $k$. The contribution is a label-free finite-grid decision, a
conditional exact search for that decision and a cost model for reuse.
Chapter 7 tests its quality and speed against fixed budgets and the
diagnostic oracle, and directly measures calibrated reuse on four cells.

The supplementary trace contents and completed strict CPU-reference
checks are documented in \cref{sec:a-supplement,sec:a-t4-t5}.
Remaining checks concern arbitrary-subset per-query invariance,
rearchiving candidate dumps for independent re-sorting, the Vogue
cross-process discrepancy and future query changes. Their status is
maintained in \path{assessment/ch7-pending-experiments.txt}.


The reported budget campaign and its trace rerun use v69 arithmetic,
including TF32 on the OpenAI datasets. The separate FP32 sensitivity
measures a search frontier; it does not recalibrate or validate these
