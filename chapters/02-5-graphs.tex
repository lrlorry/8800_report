Graph ANN replaces list scanning with navigation through promising
neighbourhoods. Its benefit is to avoid scoring most database vectors;
its GPU difficulty is the dependency between discovering a neighbour and
deciding which neighbourhood to expand. SONG separates graph search into
stages to expose distance parallelism and controls dynamic memory overhead
\cite{song}. This is an alternative access strategy, rather than a different
implementation of JHQ's fixed routed candidate universe.

CAGRA designs graph construction and search for GPU parallelism, including
small- and large-batch queries \cite{cagra}. Its graph connectivity and
search width expose a different tuning surface from IVF probes. Storing
edges and vectors creates a capacity trade-off; quantised-vector CAGRA is
also relevant, so fp32 CAGRA alone is an incomplete compressed-index
comparison. The thesis measures both in E1. Their different operating
regions are reported under the common boundary rather than inferred from
cross-paper speedup numbers.

BANG targets a different capacity regime: it keeps compressed vectors on
the GPU and accesses the host-resident graph through CPU/GPU cooperation
\cite{bang}. Capacity is obtained by distributing the working structures,
with transfer and coordination becoming part of the design. Its billion-scale
results demonstrate an alternative to complete device residency, not
capacity evidence for this thesis. The present JHQ implementation retains
its compressed search index on one device and has no comparable host-graph
execution path.

The comparison therefore turns on access regularity, representation and
residency, not a general graph-versus-quantisation hierarchy. Graph navigation
can be attractive for a small number of queries; list scans expose regular
code access and abundant batch work. Either can be bottlenecked by selection
or movement. Which wins at a target recall is an empirical question for a
specified device, memory budget and workload. E1 tests that question at batch
1,000; internal small-batch JHQ gains do not answer it for graph competitors.
