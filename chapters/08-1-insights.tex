\subsection{A useful representation exposes a useful unit of reuse}

The grouping results in \cref{fig:7-lut} show that compact state alone
does not determine the best scorer. The reusable unit is the
query-specific coordinate group: its distances are computed once and
reused across candidates, as constructed in \cref{sec:5-2}.


The shared-memory transition and word-access costs in
\cref{sec:5-2,sec:5-3} call for joint consideration of storage,
instructions and reuse. The exceptional LUT cells and Mailman case
below counter a universal smallest-table rule. Two groups are an
empirical default for this device, rather than a device-independent optimum.


The compatibility condition in \cref{sec:5-2} limits this opportunity:
improving a codebook's reconstruction can remove its execution advantage.
A representation should therefore be assessed by the attainable
recall--cost frontier of its scorer and residual hierarchy, alongside
reconstruction error.


\subsection{Useful parallelism is bounded by duplicated state and selection}

The batch trend in \cref{fig:7-batch} reflects a trade-off between
available parallelism and duplicated state/selection. Once query-level
concurrency is sufficient, staging, local buffers and merging can consume
the benefit of additional blocks. Maximum occupancy alone is therefore
the wrong objective.


Vogue's path-sensitive frontier and BGE's scheduling regret
(\cref{tab:7-e1-paths,sec:7-6}) connect execution design to the
competitive region. They also expose the limitation of CV: it measures
imbalance without absolute work or staging/merge costs. The combined
path comparison remains distinct from the shared-bound-only ablation.


A practical consequence is to retain a query-centric path and choose
grouping from both available parallelism and expected work per query.
Candidate count, survivor capacity and local-buffer cost are plausible
inputs for a richer decision. A fitted selector would need held-out
workloads and its own calibration-cost accounting; the present observations
do not validate one. The existing heuristic is valuable precisely because
its gains and failure cells can be checked separately.

\subsection{Execution speed changes the value of avoiding refinement}

Budget reduction removes residual reads, arithmetic and final selection,
while routing and broad primary-score production remain. Its potential
saving depends on the fraction of latency occupied by those removable
operations. Faster lane-cooperative refinement can consequently reduce the
reward for lowering the survivor budget. Faster primary scanning can have
the opposite effect on refinement's relative share. Neither transformation
changes the quality consequence of discarding a particular candidate.

This is a cost-model implication, rather than a causal explanation for
every historical/newer budget difference. A causal test would replay
identical budget decisions and candidate sets while changing only the
refinement kernel. The cross-campaign comparison and direct reuse
measurements are reported in \cref{sec:7-5,sec:7-stream-cost}.


The FULL working point and sampled tail risks in
\cref{tab:7-budget-full,fig:7-budget-risk} imply that calibration effort
should reflect the quality requirement and expected reuse. Optimising
the selector alone, or reporting only a favourable selected-arm mean,
leaves that engineering decision incomplete.
