\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}
JHQ-GPU demonstrates how a structured quantised hierarchy can be translated
into a useful GPU search pipeline, and why its execution and refinement
effort must be evaluated together. The four research questions now have
concrete, bounded answers.

\section{Answers to the Four Research Questions}
\label{sec:9-1}
\paragraph{RQ1: Construction and capacity.}
GPU training, batched encoding, IVF placement and packed arrays produce a
searchable resident hierarchy. The largest tested database contains
17.78 million vectors at 20.44\,GiB resident device memory; the corresponding
supplementary train-plus-add range is 10.54--10.65\,s
(\cref{sec:7-7}). Its sampled construction peak is 22.58\,GiB, while
BGE reaches 26.08\,GiB at add entry. Repeated independent builds and
startup intervals establish the measured capacity boundary more fully.

\paragraph{RQ2: Correct and efficient execution.}
The inherited Cartesian code admits exact real-arithmetic table
factorisation, and complete-key retention/merge and shared-bound arguments
explain the grouped path's selection conditions. Two-group tables lead in
22/24 measured cells. Integrated scheduling achieves $2.727\times$
geometric-mean throughput at batch 8 but only $1.015\times$ at batch 1,000
against the lane-refinement reference (\cref{sec:7-3,sec:7-6}). Thus useful
parallelism depends on batch and work distribution. Finite output checks
complement the proofs; arithmetic, tie and memory assumptions remain explicit.

\paragraph{RQ3: Refinement budget and quality.}
Unlabelled reference agreement selects workload-level survivor effort
without a learned predictor. FULL calibration meets the tested relative
recall-loss target in 12/12 cells, with maximum observed loss 0.0006.
At $S=128$, one cell instead has 40/64 threshold crossings
(\cref{sec:7-5}). The reference-based analysis explains conditional budget
search, not a population GT guarantee. Ten FULL cells still lack a
general complete-key nestedness guarantee; nevertheless all twelve FULL
finite-grid choices match enumeration. Direct calibration-plus-reuse
measurements on four cells support savings on suitable workloads and
retain the quality/time tradeoff on difficult ones.

\paragraph{RQ4: Competitive operating regions.}
At recall 0.97, the interpolated full-batch E1 comparison places JHQ first
in three datasets and near parity with IVF-RaBitQ on arXiv
(\cref{sec:7-2}). Vogue's lead depends on the grouped path. The OpenAI E1 runs use TF32;
a same-session FP32 sensitivity still exceeds archived RaBitQ by
7.9--8.6\% and CAGRA fp32 by $1.63$--$1.68\times$ at that target. CAGRA int8
wins in lower-recall regions, and IVF-RaBitQ wins at some higher targets.
The result supports a useful operating region on the measured device and
grid, alongside separately reported memory use. It supplies no cross-system
small-batch or cross-device ranking.

\section{Closing Principle and Outlook}
\label{sec:9-2}
The resulting principle is to co-evaluate representation, execution and
effort under a stated quality objective. A smaller table can add work;
more groups can duplicate it; less refinement can sacrifice the neighbours
that matter. The negative designs show why mathematical reuse and lower
reconstruction error need not improve the search frontier. The next step
is to test whether reusable calibration information can support valid risk
control at lower total cost, and whether explicit residual placement can
extend capacity without unacceptable tail latency (\cref{sec:8-4}). These
are hypotheses grounded in the measured limits, rather than capabilities
already supplied by the present system.
